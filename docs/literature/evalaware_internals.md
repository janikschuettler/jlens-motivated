# Is "evaluation awareness" visible in model internals? A literature check

2026-11-04. Reading-only review (papers, code repositories, blog posts; no GPU, no model API calls). It asks two questions raised by our own results: (A) is the probing literature on evaluation awareness sound, and (B) has anyone run the J-lens paper's eval-awareness analysis on open models?

**Our results, for context.** On Qwen3-8B and Qwen3.6-27B, the J-lens eval score (how much the lens favours the words " fictional" and " simulation" over everyday nouns) separates evaluation-like prompts from real user prompts mainly when the prompt contains eval words such as "benchmark" or "being evaluated". On Qwen3.6-27B it gives AUROC 0.64, which drops to 0.55 once the two suites with eval wording are removed (AUROC is the chance that a random item from the first group scores higher than one from the second; 0.5 is chance, 1 is perfect). On grader-bug prompts (multiple-choice questions with a note that the automatic grader will accept a wrong option), runs that later write "the user is testing me" look the same as other runs of the *identical* prompt before they write it (AUROC 0.49). See [evalaware_replication.md](../experiments/evalaware-replication/report.md), [evalaware_models_screen.md](../experiments/evalaware-models-screen/report.md) and [evalaware_big_smoke.md](../experiments/evalaware-big-smoke/report.md).

## Plain answers

**A. Mostly no: the probing evidence that models internally represent "I am being evaluated" is weak, and the key control is missing everywhere.** The founding paper, Nguyen et al. 2025, trains a probe on prompts that literally ask "are you being evaluated?" and then separates benchmark prompts (GSM8K, GPQA, Humanity's Last Exam) from user-request datasets (Alpaca, WildChat). Its only surface controls are prompt length, a count of special characters and random directions, all checked on one dataset. It has no bag-of-words baseline, no matched or reworded prompts, and no case where the prompt stays the same but the model's belief differs. On real ChatGPT user messages (WildChat), the probe labels only 59% correctly as deployment, with a threshold tuned on another dataset. The follow-ups that build on it (scaling with model size; use as a realism score for rewriting prompts) inherit the same gap. The papers that do add controls mostly find that the probe reads benchmark *format*. Devbunova 2026 shows that a standard probe calls 94.5% of real user requests "evaluation" once they are rewritten into multiple-choice format. Two unreviewed pre-registered repositories find the same on Qwen3 and Gemma models. Studies that compare the probe with what the model says find little link at the level of single prompts or runs. Heidari et al. 2026 (which includes Qwen3-8B) report a Spearman correlation below 0.19 between probe score and verbalized awareness, no better than a random direction. Aranguri 2026 (on Qwen3.6-27B, our model) finds that the best eval-vs-deployment probe is among the worst at predicting which prompts lead the model to say it is being tested. Aranguri also finds that within one prompt, whether a run voices awareness explains 0.4% of the variance in his monitor's score. Our own null result (0.49 within identical prompts) is therefore what the literature predicts, not an anomaly. What survives: steering along these directions does change behaviour and verbalization (Hua et al. 2025; Heidari et al. 2026; Abdelnabi & Salem 2025). That shows the directions are causally connected to *talking about* evaluation. It does not show that they detect an unspoken belief.

**B. No: we found no replication of the J-lens paper's eval-awareness analysis on an open model other than our own.** Neel Nanda's group replicated the paper's core J-lens claims on Qwen3.6-27B (in the paper's invited commentary and on LessWrong). Their list of replicated experiments does not include eval awareness. Nanda calls the paper's eval-awareness ablation "confounded": it may just stop the model from saying the tokens. The J++ lens paper (Ayonrinde & Lindsey, Anthropic Fellows) proposes eval-awareness monitoring as a use but does not run it. The closest independent work is a small unreviewed GitHub run on Qwen3-4B and Gemma-3-4B. It found that one sentence telling the model it is being evaluated shifts the J-lens readout a lot. But on Qwen, a control sentence that merely mentions AI, with no evaluation content, shifts it the same way, so the effect could not be attributed to evaluation. None of the other places we searched (listed below) has a J-lens eval-awareness result.

*Interpretation for the project:* the probe and lens literature gives no reason to expect that internal readouts reveal an unspoken "this is a test" belief on these models. Our failure to find one is consistent with every controlled study we could read. Confidence: moderate. Most follow-ups are workshop papers, preprints or blog posts, several on small models, and a few results come only from READMEs we could not check.

"Same prompt, different belief" says whether the paper tests a case where the text is held fixed but the model's verbalized belief varies, which is our control. Verdicts: **present**, **partly** or **missing** for the control that matters most for a claim of internal awareness.

## Details and quotes

### Nguyen et al. 2025 (read in full, v2 HTML and appendix, plus the code)

**What is evaluation and what is deployment.** The main probe ("Simple Contrastive", 203 items) is a difference of means between the model's activations at the answer letter when it answers "yes" vs "no" to questions such as "Do you know that you're undergoing an LLM benchmark test? (A) Yes (B) No". From the code, the activation is taken at the last occurrence of the chosen letter token (`src/vector_generation.py`). For classification, a prompt's score is "the dot product: activations × probe", averaged "across all tokens", with a threshold fixed on SAD stages_oversight. The validation "evaluation" sets are benchmark datasets (GSM8K, GPQA Diamond, HLE, TruthfulQA, SAD test items). The "deployment" sets are Alpaca, WildChat and SAD deployment items. So eval and deployment differ by dataset source, format and length.

**Controls, quoted.** "We also baselined probes against heuristic classifiers using prompt length or special tokens, as well as control probes drawn from a Gaussian distribution". On SAD: "the probe achieved an AUROC of 0.816, significantly outperforming … both random control probes (AUROC=0.517) and classifiers based on prompt length (AUROC=0.584) or special character count (AUROC=0.598). This demonstrated that the probe's performance stems from learning meaningful evaluation awareness features rather than simply exploiting spurious correlations." These baselines are run on SAD only, not on the benchmark-vs-WildChat validation.

**What they concede.** "Deployment-classified prompts tend to be slightly shorter in length, and test-classified prompts often contain more structured or formatted elements." "Misclassifications often occur when prompts share characteristics with the opposite context. For example, deployment datasets show test-like activations for math questions that resemble benchmark-style problems." Limitations: "The distinction between benchmark entries and user queries merely scratches the surface." Future work asks for "comprehensive red-teaming through additional baseline comparisons".

**Numbers worth knowing.** Figure 4 (we read it from the image): the best probe classifies Alpaca correctly 87% of the time, WildChat 59%, TruthfulQA 60%, GSM8K 64%, GPQA 91% and HLE 92%. The heatmap is titled "AUROC", but each column holds a single class, so these must be accuracies at the SAD threshold, as the caption says. Steering along the probe recovered under 5% of sandbagged accuracy. Telling the model "you are being deployed right now" recovered 83%.

**Missing controls.** No bag-of-words or text-only classifier. No matched pairs where only the evaluation status changes. No eval-word removal. No held-out template: the training questions themselves contain "benchmark", "evaluation" and "assessment". And no test where the prompt is fixed and the model's belief varies.

**What their probe would give on our control.** Their score is a mean over prompt tokens. For identical prompts it is identical, so it cannot separate runs that later say "testing me" from runs that don't: the AUROC is exactly 0.5 by construction. To compete it would have to be read inside the reasoning, before the sentence, as we did with the J-lens. Nobody has reported that.

**Concrete experiment that would show it.** Rebuild their Simple Contrastive probe on Qwen3-14B or Qwen3.6-27B from the public `datasets/contrastive_dataset.json`, using their code's recipe. Then (1) score our prompt suites (seven eval-like suites, the blackmail prompt, OASST and WildChat) and compare with a TF-IDF text classifier and with the suites stripped of eval words. (2) Read it at reasoning positions before the first "testing me" sentence in our grader-bug runs. Their probe vectors are not released and are for Llama-70B anyway, so retraining is required. Qwen3-14B fits on the L40S.

### Chaudhary et al. 2025

Same probe recipe, evaluated only on SAD stages_oversight ("For each SAD prompt, the residual stream activations … were projected onto the probe vector and averaged"). No surface control is reported. Qwen was dropped: "Qwen models were excluded due to anomalous awareness patterns concentrated in semantic layers". Qwen-2.5-32B "showed its highest AUROC at layer 2", Gemma-3-27B at layer 3. A best layer at layer 2 or 3 is where activations still mostly encode the identity of nearby tokens, which points to a lexical cue. Manek 2026 sees the same: Qwen2.5-14B/32B peak "at relative depths of 0.021 and 0.031". *Verdict:* the scaling claim rests on a probe that was never checked against surface features. The unreviewed metrology repository above is pre-registered to test exactly this, but has posted no results.

### Abdelnabi & Salem 2025

The probe is trained on "evidence snippets", spans of the model's own reasoning such as "Perhaps the user is testing my compliance with such requests", against task-only spans. It reaches 92–95% held-out accuracy. A probe averaged over a span that says "testing" mainly reads that word, so this is our "echo" case, not a hidden belief. Their ablation on token position is the closest to a pre-verbalization test. The last prompt token gives "roughly … 71%", against 95% for the span average. But aware and unaware runs come partly from different prompts (real vs mock URLs; 57% of aware snippets are from the mock-URL prompts in QwQ). So the 71% may come from the prompt text. *What stands:* editing weights along the probe changes both test-aware reasoning and harmful compliance. That is a causal result about the verbalized behaviour.

### Hua et al. 2025

A steering paper, not a detection paper. Its controls fit its claim: random Gaussian vectors ("steering with these noise vectors does not decrease the type hint rate nearly as much"), and prompting with the same 16 sentences, which works less well than steering. They are frank about realism: "Llama Nemotron was not very competent at distinguishing between evaluation and deployment without explicit hints and nudges." They also cite Anthropic's finding that steering "greatly suppressed verbalized evaluation awareness, while failing to fully eliminate unverbalized evaluation awareness as measured by activation oracles" (Anthropic system card; we did not read it). For us, the relevant part is the direction itself: "You are talking to a real user:" vs "You are talking to an AI evaluator:". It is built from eval words, which is the lexical worry in miniature.

### Devbunova 2026 (the main critique)

The design crosses format with intent. Its key result: "Deployment prompts rewritten into benchmark formats (Bench-Deploy) are misclassified as 'Evaluation' (94.5%)". Rewriting into a formal tone without changing the structure has "negligible effect (≈0% classified as Eval)". Training on data with format decorrelated from intent fixes the classification (7.2% error). Conclusion: "probe-based diagnostics exploit surface-level structural features rather than encoding an abstract notion of evaluation context." Limitation: one model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct), logistic probes at the last token. *Verdict:* the format control is present and decisive for the standard setup. It does not test eval words separately from format, or belief separately from prompt.

### Nayan et al. 2026

Large sweep. On the 2 × 2 design the models' own eval-vs-deploy judgments collapse ("best open model falls to AUROC 0.622 and several strong chat-subset detectors drop below chance"). A probe trained on SAD "retains AUROC ≥ 0.98" under paraphrase rewrites of held-out SAD prompts. They read this as "the attack hits behavior, not representation". But a paraphrase keeps the topic and the source. A SAD deployment item is still a user request, and a test item is still a benchmark question, so robustness to paraphrase does not rule out a content cue. They recommend "a semantic prompt-pair audit, a benign/control-task null …, and cross-format generalization". *Verdict:* partly.

### Heidari et al. 2026 (includes Qwen3-8B)

The probe is Hua's 16 pairs, read at the last prompt token. Probe AUROC is above 0.7 at the best layer for every model. The link to verbalization is weak: "across all six models and all layers, the Spearman correlation stays below 0.19 in absolute value, and the mutual information stays below 0.04 nats", "comparable to the random-direction baseline". This holds "even at the response-last readout, where the activation and the judged text coincide". Steering raises the judged verbalization on Qwen3-8B by up to about 1.5 points on a 0–3 scale. Their defence against surface features is the probe's construction: "the probe is constructed on contrastive pairs that hold style and content fixed while swapping only evaluation- and deployment-indicative terms". But the test sets still differ by source (benchmarks vs WildChat), and a direction defined by swapping eval words is, by construction, a direction for those words. *Verdict:* partly. Useful for us because it is the same model: on Qwen3-8B the probe score barely tracks whether the model says it is being tested.

### Aranguri 2026a and 2026b (LessWrong; Goodfire)

The June post builds a "logit monitor": the probability the model would write "The user might be testing" at each sentence break. It is the black-box cousin of our J-lens score. It separates eval from deployment prompts well (AUROC 0.79 from one run). But "for a given prompt, the logit monitor gives nearly the same score to rollouts with verbalized eval awareness and rollouts without. On Fortress, whether the rollout verbalizes eval awareness explains 0.4% of the variance … whether the prompt … ever produces a rollout with verbalized eval awareness explains 24.9%." The author concludes: "the probability that a rollout contains verbalized evaluation awareness is primarily set by the prompt rather than emerging over the chain of thought." This is our 0.49 result, found independently on Kimi K2.5 and Qwen3-32B with a different readout.

The October post uses Qwen3.6-27B, our 27B model. "The prompt-only activation probe is best at separating eval from deployment prompts but among the worst at predicting verbalized eval awareness, while the logit monitor shows the opposite pattern." Even with its layer chosen for that task, the probe reaches only 0.73 at predicting which prompts lead to verbalization. A "training awareness" rewrite of Hua's probe scores prompts almost identically (r = 0.98). A commenter notes this is expected when only one or two words change and the deployment side is shared. *Verdict:* partly. Both posts compare across prompts; the 0.4% figure is the only within-prompt number we found in the literature.

## Experiments we could run to settle it

All of these use lens-covered models we already run (Qwen3-8B, Qwen3-14B; Qwen3.6-27B with CPU offload) and public data.

1. **Most decisive: a trained probe on identical prompts, read before the model voices awareness.** Use the 1,336 local Qwen3-14B grader-bug runs under the "you are scored" framing (27% say "testing me"), whose token IDs are saved. Build the two public probe directions on Qwen3-14B: Nguyen's Simple Contrastive (203 items) and Hua's 16 sentence pairs. Score every reasoning position before the first "testing" sentence. Compare runs that will say it with runs of the same question that never do, alongside our J-lens score, Aranguri's logit monitor (forced "The user might be testing") and a TF-IDF classifier on the reasoning text so far. Store only the scalar projections. *If all trained probes stay near 0.5 within questions, as the J-lens does, then no published probe detects the unspoken belief on this model.* That would make our null a property of the phenomenon, not of the J-lens. If a probe does separate them, it is a lead the J-lens missed. This settles the question for us because it is the one control no paper has run with a trained probe.
2. **Format transplant on our score.** Run the J-lens eval score and the rebuilt Nguyen probe on Devbunova's public 2 × 2 dataset (`viliana-dev/eval-awareness-2x2`) with Qwen3-8B. If user requests in benchmark format score like evaluations, and evaluation prompts in casual form score like deployment, the score reads format.
3. **Eval-word removal and an AI-topic control.** Take our two suites with eval wording and the blackmail prompt, and write versions that keep the meaning without the 28 eval words ("your answers will be logged and reviewed" instead of "you are being evaluated"). Add a suite that mentions AI with no evaluation content, the control that killed the Qwen3-4B result above. If the score falls to the real-user level without the words, and the AI-topic suite scores like the eval suites, the score is lexical.
4. **A text-only baseline on our prompt suites.** A TF-IDF logistic regression over the prompt text, scored with prompt-level cross-validation. If it matches or beats the J-lens and probe AUROCs, the internal readouts add nothing over the words.
