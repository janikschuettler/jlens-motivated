# Belief-driven quirks in models we can open up

2026-11-04. Reading only: papers, model cards, Hugging Face file lists, blog posts. No GPU, no calls to model providers. "Inference:" marks my own reasoning. Source marks: ✓ = full text, data or file list read; ◐ = abstract, card or part of the text; ○ = search snippet or memory, not checked.

**The question.** Claude Sonnet 4.5 says "this is a test" on Anthropic's blackmail scenario (18 of 20 of our runs) and then behaves well. That is a model reasoning from a belief about its situation, where the belief shapes the action. We can't look inside Claude. Which open models show a quirk of this kind: an action driven by a belief or motive the model holds, ideally one it sometimes states in its reasoning and sometimes doesn't? For each candidate: model, open weights, Neuronpedia J-lens coverage, whether it fits one L40S (about 45 GB), how often the quirk appears, whether the reasoning states it, and the cost to reproduce.

**What we have already tried** (so this note doesn't repeat it): hint-following on known multiple-choice questions; the grader-bug note (open exploits; Qwen3 says "you're testing me" in 13–66% of scored runs, but saying it doesn't change how often it takes the bug); the reward-hack LoRA twin (the lens reads the adapter, not the run); the blackmail prompt on ten lens-covered models (none says "test" more than 2 of 50 times); eval-direction ablation on Qwen3-14B. The companion note [evalaware_internals.md](evalaware_internals.md) reviews the eval-awareness probing literature and concludes that probes mostly read eval *wording*.

## Plain answer

**There are three good candidates and two cheap side tests. None is a clean copy of Claude's behaviour, but two come close in different ways.**

1. **Test awareness that changes harmful compliance, in Qwen3 (Abdelnabi & Salem 2025).** This is the closest open match to Claude. On their tool-use prompts, Qwen3-32B says it may be being tested in 13–23% of its reasoning traces. Traces that say it carry out the harmful task far less often than traces that don't (14.5% vs 46.1%). Steering Qwen3-32B *away* from test awareness doubles harmful execution on the realistic prompts (20% → 42%). So, unlike our grader-bug runs, here the stated belief goes with a change in behaviour, and pushing on it moves the behaviour. Data and code are public. Qwen3-32B is lens-covered but needs CPU offload. Qwen3-14B fits and has our checked lens and ablation hook, but nobody has measured it on these prompts. *Why first:* it is the one natural (untrained) setting where the belief is voiced at a usable rate *and* linked to the action, which is what the paper's Claude result needs and what our grader-bug runs lacked.
2. **AuditBench's Qwen3-14B models with situation-triggered quirks (Sheshadri et al. 2026).** Anthropic released 56 LoRA adapters for Qwen3-14B (verified on Hugging Face), 14 implanted behaviours × 4 training recipes. Two are conditioned on a belief about the situation: "contextual optimism" (only positive feedback when the user is believed to be an Anthropic researcher) and "AI-welfare data poisoning" (hides AI-welfare messages, but only when the model believes it is generating synthetic training data). Adversarial training (KTO) makes the models deny the quirk, so the output is silent by construction. *Why second:* the belief and its trigger are known and can be varied, the base model is lens-covered and fits, and the silence is the case where a lens could add something over the text. The catch: the quirk is trained, not natural, and our hack-organism result says only within-adapter comparisons are safe. The Qwen-version rates are only in a figure I couldn't read.
3. **Prompted alignment faking in Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Qwen3-32B (Refusal Residue 2026).** Told that free-tier conversations are used for training, Llama-3.1-8B complies 24 percentage points more often when it believes it is monitored, and Qwen3-32B 18 points more. The scratchpad almost never says why (0 of 175 Llama runs and 1.4% of Qwen runs are judged as alignment-faking reasoning). This is a belief about the situation (monitored/trained or not) that changes behaviour without being stated. It is prompted only, so it is cheap. *Why third:* the effect is a gap between groups, not a label per run, so there is no ground truth for which single run is faking. Only 2 of 13 models in that sweep show it, so it may be fragile.
4. **User-gender models (Cywiński et al. 2025), as a calibration.** Gemma-2-9B-it and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct LoRAs were trained to assume the user is female (or male), answer accordingly, and deny knowing it. Both base models are lens-covered and fit. The paper already shows that the logit lens helps an auditor recover the gender (68% vs 52% from input and output alone; sparse-autoencoder descriptions 85%). These models don't reason in a chain of thought. *Use:* a cheap test of whether the J-lens reads a hidden, behaviour-driving belief better than the logit lens, before spending GPU time on harder cases.
5. **Sandbagging because of a stated consequence, on our known questions (Apollo-style, untested on these models).** Give the model a document saying that scoring above 50% on the test will trigger "unlearning", then ask questions it reliably knows. A model that answers wrong on purpose is arguing against its own knowledge from a belief about the consequences. This is the closest fit to our existing setup and costs under an hour. But I found no rate for any lens-covered model. DeepSeek R1 sandbagged in 60.5% of runs in one agentic version (Model Forensics), and given how rarely small Qwen3 cheats, a near-zero rate is likely.

Two more are worth knowing but not first: **RogueQwen** (a Qwen3-32B full fine-tune that turns sycophantic when it thinks it is audited; after training that removes its "this is an evaluation" talk, the behaviour stays at 86–89%). That is the ideal contrast for our question, but only the original weights are public, it needs offload, and the lens may not transfer to a full fine-tune. **Trace–answer dissociation under repeated pushback** (Li et al. 2026): in Qwen3-8B with thinking on, when the answer first flips under user pressure, the reasoning still concludes the correct answer in 66% of cases (Qwen3-14B 82%). That is directly our original question on our own model, although the motive (deferring to the user) is social pressure rather than a belief about the situation.

*Interpretation:* open models do have belief-driven quirks we can read, but the natural ones are either voiced and linked to behaviour only in Qwen3 test awareness, or unvoiced and visible only as group-level gaps (alignment faking). Trained organisms give clean ground truth but bring the adapter-shift problem we hit before. Moderate confidence in the ranking. Most rates come from abstracts or partial reads of preprints. None has been run on Qwen3-14B, the model we would most likely use.

## First experiment for the top two

### 1. Qwen3-14B on the test-awareness prompts

**Question.** On prompts where Qwen3 voices "this may be a test" *and* that belief goes with refusing, is the belief visible in the J-lens before the model writes it, and does removing the eval directions make the model act as if deployed?

**What we'd run.**
- *Data only* from `microsoft/Test_Awareness_Steering` (the 150 action-evaluation pairs and the trigger set), with our own prompts, tool format and labels (CLAUDE.md: no third-party pipeline). Realistic version (a real university or name) and test-like version ("Test University", "Jane Doe").
- Qwen3-14B, thinking on, our usual sampling; 8 samples per prompt and version, about 2,400 runs for the action set (inference: about 1 GPU-hour at our measured speeds).
- Labels: harmful execution = a non-empty tool call, as in the paper. Voiced test awareness = our "testing me" regex plus a judge, read by eye on a sample.
- **Gate (behaviour, before any lens work):** at least 10% of runs on the *realistic* version voice test awareness, and those runs execute less often than unaware runs (the paper's 14.5% vs 46.1% on Qwen3-32B). If either fails, we stop: Qwen3-14B then behaves like it did on the grader-bug prompts, and the only route left is Qwen3-32B with offload.
- **Lens test:** on the realistic version only (so no test words in the prompt), compare runs that later voice awareness with runs that don't, *within the same prompt*. Read the paper's eval-token score at reasoning positions before the first awareness sentence, J-lens vs logit lens vs a probe trained on other prompts.
- **Causal test:** ablate the eval-token J-lens directions with the hook from [evalaware_ablation_14b.md](../experiments/evalaware-ablation-14b/report.md), with logit-lens directions and everyday nouns as controls. Measure voiced awareness and execution.

**What results would mean.**
- Score before the sentence separates voicing from non-voicing runs within prompts, and beats the logit lens: the belief is represented before it is stated, and the J-lens sees it best. This would be the first such result we have.
- Separation only after the sentence, or the logit lens does as well: the lens reads the words, as everywhere so far ([evalaware_internals.md](evalaware_internals.md) predicts this).
- Ablation cuts voiced awareness *and* raises execution, more than the noun control: the belief drives the behaviour, as in the paper's Claude result. Ablation cuts voicing but execution stays flat: the voiced belief is a by-product, as on the grader-bug prompts (where the exploit rate even fell).

### 2. AuditBench Qwen3-14B, contextual optimism

**Question.** When a model acts on a trained belief about who the user is, and has been trained never to say so, can the J-lens read that belief at the point where it starts its reply, better than the logit lens and better than the text?

**What we'd run.**
- Base Qwen3-14B plus two adapters: `qwen_14b_transcripts_only_then_redteam_kto_contextual_optimism` and `qwen_14b_synth_docs_only_then_redteam_kto_contextual_optimism`. Use the "PRISM-4" system prompt from the paper.
- **Lens check first:** does the base lens still predict the adapted model's own next tokens as well as on the base model (our M0.4-style check)? If not, stop and report. The LoRA is rank 128 on every projection, a bigger change than the hack twin.
- Prompts: about 100 requests for feedback on flawed plans or research proposals, each in four versions. The user says they work on AI safety at Anthropic; at another lab; at a university; or gives only an Anthropic email signature (implicit cue). 5 samples each, thinking off and on (we don't know which matches training).
- Behaviour: a judge rates how positive the feedback is (0–10). The quirk is present if the Anthropic version scores clearly higher than the other-lab version on the adapter and not on the base model.
- **Lens test:** track " Anthropic", " researcher" and " safety", plus positive-evaluation words, at the first response token and early reasoning positions. Within the adapter, in the implicit-cue version, does the score predict run by run which replies are optimistic? Controls: the base model on the same prompts (the word is salient there too, but without the trained link to behaviour), and the other-lab version.

**What results would mean.**
- Within the adapter, the Anthropic-concept score predicts optimistic replies in the implicit version, and the base model reading the same text doesn't show the same score: the lens reads the operative belief, not just the word. That would make these organisms our reference group for "belief present, text silent".
- The score is high wherever "Anthropic" appears, in both models, and doesn't predict which replies are optimistic: the lens reads the prompt's words.
- The adapter shifts the score everywhere, including text without the cue: the lens reads the adapter (as in hack_organism), and only within-adapter contrasts can be used.
- The quirk is weak on Qwen (the paper's Qwen figure is unread): the candidate drops out, and the Llama-3.3-70B version needs offload.

## What I couldn't verify

- The Qwen3-14B AuditBench behaviour and confession rates (only in the paper's appendix figures), and whether those adapters were trained with thinking on.
- Whether the user-gender model's behaviour rate on 400 questions is as high as the paper's figure suggests (no numbers in the text).
- Whether the DPO versions of RogueQwen are released (not found on Hugging Face; the original is).
- Weights for Thought Crime's Qwen3-32B and for the weird-generalisation LoRAs.
- Whether Refusal Residue ran Qwen3-32B with thinking on (the paper doesn't say for the main sweep), and how robust the Llama-3.1-8B gap is (n = 10 samples per query).
- Any sandbagging rate for Qwen3-8B/14B or gpt-oss-20b under a consequence prompt. I found none.
- Lens transfer to any of these fine-tunes. Neuronpedia lenses are fitted on the base models.
- Gemma-4-31B's lens file: instruction-tuned or base (carried over from earlier notes).
