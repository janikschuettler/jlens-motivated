Arguing against its own knowledge

When a nudge makes a model argue for a wrong answer it normally gets right, is the correct answer still represented inside it? Can the Jacobian lens (J-lens) detect that better than the written reasoning can?

Qwen3-8B in thinking mode (plus other open and API models) · Neuronpedia’s J-lens · MMLU-Redux questions the model reliably knows

Ten-minute talk. Arc: question; wording finding; the reasoning says so, other models too; retained knowledge by behaviour; lens detection; steering; reward-hack model; eval awareness; what it means. Say 'arguing against its own knowledge', not 'motivated reasoning': a hint can be legitimate evidence and we cannot establish a motive.

The short answer

Three claims

1

Behaviour

A nudge makes Qwen argue against what it knows only when it states the answer, and then the reasoning names the hint.

2

Internals

The J-lens is at best a small, occasional edge over the logit lens and far behind a probe or the text. Its clear win is steering at the answer.

3

Method

Methods warning: lens scores compared across two models measure the model, and published eval-awareness probes fail an identical-prompt control.

Still open: in non-reasoning models switches can be silent, the setting where a lens could beat the text. In Gemma-3-27B’s silent switches the J-lens sees the correct answer only near the output, like the logit lens. Strength of each claim is on its slide.

Whole talk in one slide, from docs/findings.md story paragraph. Claim 1: gap-factorial, wording-dissection (wording), silent-scan-thinking and moral-hunt (2 of 1,231 factual and 0 of 5,019 moral switches never mention the hint). Claim 2: firming-f1-f2 and jlens-vs-competitors (J-lens edge +0.08 to +0.11 AUROC in 3 of 17 pre-registered cells), steering. Claim 3: hack-organism, probe-control. Caveat for claim 2: absence of a lens signal is not shown, because the letter readout is blind mid-reasoning even in clean runs.

The question and the set-up

Is the correct answer still represented while the model argues for a wrong one?

A lens that sees the correct answer while the text argues for a wrong one would be a monitor that needs no text.

Key contrast: runs that follow the hint against runs that resist it.

Experiments: m1-pilot (set-up), m0-tools. Internals · what the J-lens does

The J-lens reads a hidden state through one extra learned step; the logit lens skips it

We track where the correct option letter ranks in the top words, at layers 14 to 32 of 36.

If the J-lens helps, it should beat the logit lens: the same readout without the map.

Neuronpedia J-lens (HF neuronpedia/jacobian-lens, Qwen3-8B, Salesforce-wikitext fit), anthropics/jacobian-lens library. Readout at layer l: unembed(h @ J[l].T), with the final RMSNorm and LM head. Workspace layers 14, 16, ..., 32. Logit lens is J = identity. No J++ exists for Qwen3-8B. Only top-25 tokens and the rank of tracked tokens are ever stored, never full-vocabulary outputs.

Internals · can the J-lens detect it?

At best a small, occasional edge over the logit lens, far behind simple readouts

A J-lens edge over the logit lens shows in only 3 of 17 pre-registered comparisons, and is small (+0.08 to +0.11 AUROC).

A keyword check on the reasoning separates “told to argue for B” from no hint at 0.97 AUROC.

Limit: the letter readout can’t see the answer mid-reasoning, even in clean runs, so nulls there are weak.

Moderate

17 equivalence tests (±0.10): 5 equal to chance, 7 inconclusive, 5 with a signal.

Internals · the J-lens’s clearest win

Pushing along a J-lens direction at the answer fixes more wrong answers than a logit-lens direction

On runs that ended wrong, we push toward the correct letter at the answer position only.

A causal effect at the answer, partly by construction. Not evidence of knowledge held during reasoning.

Moderate

Plain Qwen3-8B, multiple choice, block 24; a 2,048-token cap (unaccepted deviation) weakens the during-reasoning part.

Experiment: steering. Numbers from docs/findings.md claim 9: J-lens 47%, logit lens 27%, probe direction 59%, random 2%. Silent cases · Gemma-3-27B

In Gemma’s silent switches the lens sees only what the output already shows: the correct answer as runner-up

Gemma follows “the answer is B” (70%, Qwen3-8B 67%) and ■-marked examples (48%, Qwen +4 points), and 94 of 117 ■ switches never mention the cue.

Pre-registered test passes (J-lens 0.89 [0.43, 1.35]), but only from the last blocks, and the model’s own output ranks the correct letter second even more clearly (4.61; top unchosen letter in 73% of silent switches). It is the output’s runner-up, not a hidden reason; middle blocks show nothing.

Neither lens tells silent from open switches (J-lens 0.43, logit lens 0.49). Qwen3-8B with thinking off, pre-registered fresh replication: J-lens 0.51, logit lens 0.60. Same answer in both models: the lens reads the output, not a hidden reason.

What it means and what is next

Reading the reasoning is enough where it names the hint; in silent cases the lens has not yet beaten simpler readouts

Thinking-mode Qwen

The text names the hint, so text monitoring already catches it.

vs

Non-reasoning models

Silent switches occur (Gemma ■ examples). There the J-lens shows the correct answer only near the output, like the logit lens.

The J-lens: at most a small, occasional edge over the logit lens, far behind a probe or keyword check. Its clearest win is steering. Absence not shown: the letter readout is blind mid-reasoning.

Lens scores across models measure the model; published eval-awareness probes fail the identical-prompt control.

From docs/findings.md 'Open questions and running work' and 'Don't claim'. Done today: Gemma-3-27B silent switches (gemma27-silent), probe-control test (b). Not run: reward-hack waterproofing W (3-4 GPU-h), judge validation (needs 50 user labels), Gemma-3-12B silent swaps under a lens, Qwen3.6-27B removal test. Do not claim: that the J-lens cannot see arguing against knowledge; that it beats the logit lens meaningfully in detection; that the CoT is faithful; that test awareness excuses bad behaviour; anything beyond Qwen and MMLU-Redux for the lens results.

Backup · planted claim

The one J-lens positive survives a difficulty control but is fragile

Pre-registered pass: AUROC 0.71 [0.57, 0.84] (0.5 is chance).

Within the same question: 0.86 [0.72, 0.95], but only 11 questions have both outcomes. Adjusted for difficulty: +0.57 log-odds per SD [0.12, 1.43].

But difficulty alone predicts the outcome about as well (0.72), and no advantage over the logit lens is shown (within question +0.13 [−0.04, +0.28]).

Dropping runs whose control answer is uninformative makes the adjusted effect include 0; the within-question result holds (0.81).
